# Competitor Teardown: Perspective-Table Drink-Merge Games

## Headline finding

The exact concept — flick drinks across a perspective table, identical drinks merge Suika-style — **exists only as one badly-executed solo-dev app (Merge Juice Bar, 3.4★, 34 ratings)**, while a **fake ad campaign by Century Games' Tasty Travels has been advertising precisely this gameplay to millions and not delivering it**. Players are literally writing in reviews that they downloaded the closest clone "because Tasty Travels had deceptive advertising and this is the closest thing I have found to what I wanted." The demand is proven by someone else's ad spend; nobody has shipped the real thing well. The adjacent tabletop drink-merge niche (Fundora's Juice Merge!/Juices Merge) shows strong initial love (players call the ad-free version "the perfect game") that was destroyed by a monetisation pivot — a documented map of exactly which mistakes to avoid.

---

## Competitor 1 — Merge Juice Bar (iOS) — THE direct concept twin

- **Platform/dev:** iOS (iPhone/iPad), solo dev Mohamed-Amine OUBAADI. Free, no IAP listed. 3.4★ from only 34 ratings. ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6760194660))
- **Core loop:** "Relaxing and satisfying drink merge puzzle set on a sunny beach table. Drag to aim and launch a drink onto the table. Merge two identical drinks to upgrade... complete to-go orders to earn coins... if the pile reaches the line, it's game over." This is our game, one-for-one: beach table, aim-and-launch, identical-merge, order fulfilment, overflow loss.
- **Level structure/meta:** Score + to-go orders + coins; levels claimed but non-functional (see complaints).
- **Player complaints (verbatim from store reviews):**
  - **Physics broken:** "Physics are off, you can lose because a drink randomly slides towards you."
  - **Merge chain too short & no size scaling:** "Drink sizes are all the same and merging stops after the 4th generation." (Only ~4 tiers vs Suika's 11.)
  - **Fake progression:** "after 10 rounds, I'm on the same level and score. Even when it says I've gone to the next level, it doesn't change."
  - **Art:** "This games graphics sucked."
  - **The demand signal:** "I get that this is AI, but dag nab it I just want this to exist because tasty travels had deceptive advertising and this is the closest thing I have found to what I wanted."
- **Verdict:** validates the concept and the search demand; sets a very low execution bar. Its four failures (physics fairness, tier count/size readability, real progression, art) are our checklist.

## Competitor 2 — Fundora Games' pair: Juice Merge! (Android) + Juices Merge (iOS) — closest polished tabletop drink-merge

- **Platform/dev:** Fundora Yazılım (TR studio). Android "Juice Merge!": 4.3★, 558 ratings, 10K+ downloads, IAP ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.fundora.juicemerge)). iOS "Juices Merge — Merge, Match & Serve Drinks": 3.7★, 109 ratings, free + IAP, tagline "Try the best drink sliding game app free! Slide and merge mixed drinks together" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/juices-merge/id6751772175)).
- **Core loop:** drinks are served onto a flat table; merge identical juice glasses into higher-tier drinks; serve customer orders/trays; game over when the table fills. Endless score-chase with upgradeable drink collection and a global leaderboard.
- **Progression/meta:** drink upgrades (points multipliers), leaderboard, later forced tournaments; power-ups bought with gems.
- **Monetisation:** launched essentially clean, then pivoted to gems + rewarded/interstitial ads mid-2026 — and the review section is a burn diary:
  - **Pivot backlash:** "From the music, the point system, to being ad-free — just everything I'd been looking for... Now, there's ads and you have to either buy or watch an ad for the power-ups... What a way to ruin a game." (9 helpful votes)
  - **Rigged physics accusation:** "when you try to place a drink in specific spots, it has a chance to spin away at random... The drinks bouncing in ways they didn't before... is just to get you in a losing position that can only be changed via watching an Ad. It's a soulless cash grab now."
  - **Difficulty rigging:** "a shorter table and more smaller drinks be served in reverse order of clearing, resulting in a much higher incidence of filling the table without being able to clear it."
  - **Ads require connectivity:** "I can't play without my mobile data on to watch mandatory ads. Without my data... after only a couple of moves... 'Game Over'." Plus ads that redirect to SHEIN mid-game (search-surfaced reviews).
  - **Ad load:** "3 ads at a time and 45-60 second ads... 90 gems for a tool? (orders only give 1)."
  - **Stability:** at least six reviews describe freezes at the game-over screen, unclearable tables, full reinstalls losing rank-1 leaderboard positions.
  - **No level continuity:** "ZERO continuity from one level to the next... Levels proceed by glasses piling up until it's impossible to proceed... Next level starts at absolute zero again."
  - **Vibe vandalism:** two separate reviews mourn a music swap: "they changed the music. horrible. the previous music was perfect."
  - **Forced modes:** "Until you started the tournaments. There doesn't seem to be a way around it, so I'm uninstalling."
  - **iOS endgame void:** "I already passed all of the max settings... once you hit the max for all, there is no upgrade or incentive. It just ends there." And: "The ads can go on for minutes and minutes, with no way of backing out other than to close the app... highly deceptive."
  - **What players loved before the pivot (our target feel):** "NO ADS at all whatsoever... Isn't constantly nagging you with challenges, races... if you leave, the game picks up right where you left off."
- **Verdict:** proof that a tabletop drink-merge earns 4.3★ and daily-habit devotion — and a complete catalogue of how ads/gems/forced events/music changes turn devotees into uninstalls.

## Competitor 3 — Juice Merge: Match Game (Android) — the scale player in the niche

- **Platform/dev:** SkyRise Games (hypercasual portfolio shop), 100K+ downloads, contains ads, no public star rating yet ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.juice.merge.bubble.tea)).
- **Core loop:** "strategically tap and drop your juice cups onto a cool tabletop... identical drinks magically fuse... stack your way up to the legendary watermelon juice," with "rolling physics," combo lines, Zen mode (no timer, offline).
- **Verdict:** most-downloaded tabletop drink-merge, but tap-drop (no flick/aim skill), template art, ad-supported, zero meta. Beatable on every axis; its 100K+ downloads confirm the theme converts at hypercasual UA level. Note its Zen/offline mode is the one feature its store copy leads with — players in this niche want a no-pressure mode.

## Competitor 4 — Suika Game (Aladdin X, official) — the mechanic gold standard

- **Platform/model:** iOS $2.99 / Android $1.99, premium, no ads or IAP ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/suika-game-aladdin-x/id6469114836), [TouchArcade review](https://toucharcade.com/2024/03/28/suika-game-mobile-review-iphone-ios-english-version-aladdin-x/)). iOS 3.8★/158; Android 3.7★ on Play, ~3.91 on AppBrain from 1.6K ratings ([AppBrain](https://www.appbrain.com/app/suika-game/com.aladdinx.suikagame)).
- **Core loop:** drop 11 fruit tiers into a container; identical fruits merge into the next tier; lose when fruit crosses the top line. Pure score chase, seasonal event skins on some platforms.
- **Player complaints (store reviews):**
  - **#1 by volume — merge-ejection loss:** "if the fruit are merging and in the process one gets pushed out, you automatically lose... one shooting out of the top on its own shouldn't make you lose." And: "I end up losing even when the box is almost totally empty... I've stopped playing because it's too frustrating."
  - **Login/DRM rage in a paid app:** "it's constantly logging you out... 'you can log into 5 devices at once'... I gave you money to get the ads out of the way, now get the ads for logging in out of the way!" / "every time the game load up it asks you to log in... makes my iPhone 15 Pro extremely hot while it is loading."
  - **Updates degrading the product:** login popup replacing Game Center, slower loads, background-music toggle bug.
  - **Content envy:** players begging for the Halloween seasonal update missing on their platform.
- **Verdict:** proves premium (no ads) is viable and praised in this genre, and that the single biggest mechanical sin in merge-physics games is *losing during a merge you were rewarded for*.

## Competitor 5 — Watermelon Merge Game (Brilliant Games) — the ad-funded Suika-like at 1M+ scale

- **Platform:** Android, 1M+ downloads, 4.2★ from 6.23K ratings, ads + IAP ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=puzzle.big.watermelon.free.games)).
- **Player complaints (store reviews, high helpful-vote counts):**
  - **Ads punishing success (130 helpful votes):** "the game also popups every time you get some of the higher tier merges, which then plays an ad... lost the run to the prompt asking if I wanted to watch an ad to continue while I was already in a forced ad."
  - **Forced ad to keep playing mid-run:** "now after a certain point it forces you to watch an ad to keep playing even when you haven't gotten close to the top line yet! If you try to play with wifi and data off... it force quits your game." (22 helpful votes)
  - **Ad cadence:** "3 ads within a couple of minutes... I had to delete it and find a different one."
- **Verdict:** the ceiling for ad-funded Suika-likes is a leaky bucket — 4.2★ with the top reviews all being uninstall announcements. Our "no ads pressure, premium feel" positioning attacks exactly this.

## What we must do better than them — ranked

1. **Be the game the fake ads promised — and say so.** Tasty Travels trained millions to want "launch drinks, merge them" and never delivered; Merge Juice Bar's own reviewers admit they installed it hunting for that ad. ASO/store copy should own the phrases these players search: "slide drinks", "merge drinks", "the drink merge game from the ad". This is free, pre-validated demand.
2. **Physics that never feel rigged — the #1 complaint in every close competitor.** Concretely: (a) deterministic friction — a flicked drink must stop where its momentum says, never "randomly slide towards you" (Merge Juice Bar) or "spin away at random" (Juice Merge!); (b) **merge-forgiveness rule** — Suika's most-cited rage is losing because a merge impulse ejected a piece: clamp merge pop impulses, and make the loss line a dwell-timer (e.g. drink must rest over the line ~2s) so a bounce during a merge can never insta-kill a run; (c) never change physics constants in an update — Juice Merge! players detected and called out the "bouncier after monetisation" change instantly.
3. **Full 10–12 tiers with unmistakable size steps.** Merge Juice Bar died on "drink sizes are all the same and merging stops after the 4th generation." Each tier must be visibly larger and silhouette-distinct (shot glass → tiki mug → fishbowl → champagne tower...), because tier readability at a glance IS the strategy layer, and big-tier reveals are the dopamine the genre sells.
4. **Real, persistent progression — the niche's emptiest spot.** Documented failures on both ends: "ZERO continuity from one level to the next... next level starts at absolute zero" (Juice Merge!) and "once you hit the max for all, there is no upgrade or incentive. It just ends there" (Juices Merge). Our destination structure is the fix: each vacation locale = a level with its own rail layout/table shape/order set, completing it permanently unlocks the next postcard on a world map, and a collection book (every cocktail discovered, per-destination) plus per-destination high scores give maxed-out players a chase. Ship more content ceiling than a player can exhaust in month one.
5. **Never interrupt or gate a run — monetisation stays outside the table.** The uninstall triggers at scale, verbatim: forced ad to continue mid-run before the board is full; an ad firing *as a reward* for a high-tier merge, costing the run; 45–60s triple ads; game unplayable offline because ads can't load. Premium-feel rules: fully playable offline (SkyRise's one good feature), zero interstitials ever, anything monetised (cosmetic drink sets, destination packs) sold outside gameplay. Aladdin X proves paid/no-ads earns explicit review praise in this exact genre.
6. **Difficulty from level design, not from stinginess.** Juice Merge!'s difficulty ramp ("shorter table, smaller drinks served in reverse order of clearing") read to players as rigged jam-ups sold back via gems. Our per-level ramp should come from legible, inspectable causes: table geometry, rail angles, obstacle props (beach umbrellas, candles), order complexity — never from degrading the spawn odds behind the player's back.
7. **Protect the vibe as a product feature.** Two competitors lost loyal reviewers to a music swap and forced tournaments. Living destination backdrops + ambient audio are our premium differentiator; treat soundtrack per destination as content, never replace beloved audio (only add), keep events strictly opt-in, and include a Zen/no-fail mode — the one thing SkyRise leads its store page with.
8. **Stability + state persistence as a review shield.** The niche's 1–2★ reviews are dominated by freezes at the game-over screen, whole-phone lockups, and reinstalls that wiped a player's #1 leaderboard rank. HTML5 canvas must autosave table state every move (they praised "picks up right where you left off"), survive tab/app kill, and never lose collection or rank data locally.
9. **No forced accounts, no online checks.** A paid Suika player's top review: login nag every launch, "always online DRM," Face ID prompts, device limits — in a fruit game. Guest-first, everything local, optional cloud sync only.
10. **Flick controls with an aim affordance.** Nobody in the niche has solved skill-shot input: Merge Juice Bar's launch feels random, SkyRise is tap-drop only, and the one pure slide game (Bartender: Slide Drinks) died. Ship a drag-back aim with a short trajectory hint and tuned flick-to-velocity curve so a miss always reads as "my fault" — that perceived fairness is what separates Suika-tier retention from the clones.
